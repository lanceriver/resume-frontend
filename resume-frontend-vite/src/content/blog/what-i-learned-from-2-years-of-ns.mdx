It's been exactly 523 days since my first post on this site, back when I was still donning the green uniform of the SAF. I like how I told myself this would be a good idea, an outlet for me to focus my energy on while going through the daily monotony of life in the army. Unfortunately, like too many of us can attest to, I never followed up. It's really easy to say "Oh, I'll make this a habit" but actually following through is a whole other ballgame. So anyways, what's new in my life? I'm back in Sydney, back in uni doing my CS degree, grinding for internships. It's been about 6 months since I finished NS, and the freedom of being a civilian again has its pros and cons. I've had a lot of time to reflect on the 2 years, a time that honestly feels like a lifetime away now. So here's my 2 cents on the whole experience.

## It's All About Luck

Like many things in life, your NS experience is largely based on factors out of your control. This starts with the medical grade or "PES" grade they give you at the check-up before service. It may seem pretty structured but their grading system is known to have a few cracks. I've known people to have various health issues such as asthma, severe myopia, get a "PES A" grading, which allows them to be assigned to any combat vocation, while those with a history of past injuries (maybe they broke their arm in Year 3) get a "PES C" grading, allowing them to be assigned to "easier" vocations. How your grade gets assigned exactly is a mystery, so that's the first aspect of luck. You'll see a pattern emerge throughout your service, from the buddies you're assigned to the actual job you'll be doing for the 2 years. One guy might be out in the jungle on a 5 day outfield mission, while his best mate who enlisted with him is watching Netflix in an air-conditioned office. You might be assigned guard duty to stay in camp on a public holiday while all your buddies go home and enjoy a long weekend. Maybe that day the food at the cookhouse was way less edible than usual. The point I'm making is that as a "loyal servant" to the government for two years you don't have a lot of agency in these matters, so your 2 years are out of your control, unless you act on it. If you don't like your situation, you'll have to "create your own luck". A quick read of the [NS subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/NationalServiceSG/) will show you what I mean by this. I was very lucky to have very reasonable superiors who made my environment similar to that of a corporate one you might find in the civilian world, but getting to this point required me to create my own luck.

## Don't Stand Out If You Don't Need To

I had an interaction with this uncle at VivoCity mall the day before I enlisted which really imprinted in me. He was running an eyeglass shop which I was visiting to get new glasses for enlistment - I didn't want to ruin my nice glasses with all the running and getting dirty I knew we were going to do. When I mentioned I was going into NS we had a nice long chat about the whole thing, a recurring conversation that I had with various taxi drivers throughout the 2 years. Since I just got back from 6 years in Australia I was scared and uncertain of what to expect, which I shared with him. He seemed like he was in his 60s, so I kind of expected him to rattle off the same boomer schtick "Man up lah", "Back in my time ah, we didn't even have this", "This generation everything so easy ah why need to complain". Instead he gave me this absolute gem:

> Boy, if you don't want to go OCS or SCS, stay in the middle. Don't be a *wayang king*, but also don't be a *blur cock*.

For those of us not versed in Singlish, let me explain. A *wayang king* is someone who sucks up to his superiors, a know-it-all who tries to appear more impressive than he actually is. A *blur cock* is the opposite, a person so incompetent he drags down everyone around him. OCS and SCS are officer and sergeant training schools for those who want to become commanders. As an aside, the film [Ah Boys to Men](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KTWie1WHTW4) does an excellent job of portraying these characters, as well as NS life in basic training. But those words really stuck out to me. I had no interest in "honour" or "glory" or any kind of leadership role as a commander. From what my friends who already went in were telling me, it was extra work for not so extra pay. As far as I was concerned, I was here to do my 2 years and go back to Australia. So I went into basic training with his advice in my mind, and sure enough, I saw plenty of *wayang kings* and *blur cocks*. The biggest *wayang king* was in Section 3, and **man** did everyone hate him. He was constantly asking the sergeants if they needed help, feigning laughter at their jokes when it really wasn't that funny! - berating us in front of them for small infractions like not tucking in your shirt, which I doubt they would've noticed if he didn't point it out. There's a difference between following rules and just wanting to f--k over your buddies. He was clearly pretty good at the whole soldiering thing, and wow did he make everyone know it! If someone even dared to ask him a question, like how to apply camo properly, all we'd hear was "Didn't Sergeant XXX teach you this already? You go ask him lah!" Like does it really hurt to take a few minutes of your time and help a brother out? Then there was the *blur cock*. This guy was in my section, and wow did he make life interesting. For one - he was **allergic** to personal hygiene. We were supposed to change bedsheets every 2 weeks, and this brother changed it *twice* in the 4 months we were all together. After PT (Physical Training) or any activity where we had to get dirty and muddy, isn't it logical or simply hygienic to wash up before going on your bed? This brother did none of that, just lay down in bed muddy uniform be damned. Safe to say that the room stank! Of course, we tried to help out. We wanted to make him realise and teach him, maybe he just had no concept of it. But that's where we all got annoyed: **He didn't want to listen.** We tried to tell him nicely, but nothing ever got through to him. It didn't stay at hygiene, he was constantly late, which made the sergeants furious at us. His bearings (uniform standards) were always below par, and his marching was constantly out of time. All of this caused us to be punished frequently, with pushups, runs around the block, among others. We weren't mad at him for being *blur*, most of us recruits were - but the lack of accountability on his part is what did him in. So I guess that's where *cock* comes in for *blur cock*. As for me, I stayed out of trouble. I did stick out a little because of my background coming from Australia and all that, but for the most part I did what I had to do.

## Being Good Is Not Enough

At the end of basic training every company votes for a *Company Best*, an award given to the soldier who displayed the most leadership and competent skills, a lock to be sent to OCS to become an officer. The guy who won was from my section, and he was basically who the *wayang king* wanted to be. Except, instead of berating he advised, he looked out for all of us in the section and the platoon. He was a natural with rifle handling and outfield training, which the other guy was too, but it's not enough to be good - you have to be liked too.
